## Transfer notes (methodological)
- Time-space (connection) network with flight arcs, ground arcs and wrap-around count constraints (Rushmeier-Kontogiorgis; Rexing et al.; Lohatepanont-Barnhart) is the representation the RAM flow problem inherits; charging adds an energy coordinate (Kinene-Birolini).
- Flight copies / time windows (Rexing; Lan-Clarke-Barnhart; Jiang-Barnhart; Marla-Vaaben-Barnhart) discretise departure-time and speed choice, the same device ATFM ground-holding models use and the device launch-window scheduling needs.
- Recovery models (Rosenberger et al.; Petersen et al.; Maher; Lee-Marla-Jacquillat; Cadarso-Vaze) supply set-packing/Benders/column-and-row generation patterns for rolling-horizon re-planning under airport capacity uncertainty.
- Speed-control recovery (Akturk et al.; Arikan et al.; Marla et al.) gives conic/MIP treatments of the energy-vs-block-time tradeoff that RAM electric aircraft face.
- Robust routing and delay propagation (Lan et al.; Dunbar et al.; Yan-Kung; Froyland-Maher-Wu; Rosenberger 2004) provide slack-allocation and structural-robustness logic for tight turnaround/charging buffers.
- Hub-capacity-aware schedule design (Pita et al.; Bayram et al.; Lederer-Nambimadom) connects the facet to hub-and-spoke feeder design for Topic B.
