## Transfer notes for the spaceport-to-orbit feeder review
- Freeman et al. (1966) is the earliest OR formulation of launch manifesting: given time-phased demand at an orbital/lunar
  hub, choose the number of resupply trips, their dates and each cargo mix — exactly the spoke-to-hub manifest problem
  with capacitated vehicles; worth citing as the root of the lineage before the time-expanded-network work of Ho/de Weck.
- Fisher & Jaikumar (1978) is the archetypal launch-scheduling model: select launch times for a set of missions to
  minimise the number flown late under earliest-start and shared-resource (orbiter, pad, processing facility) constraints;
  maps to assigning payloads to launch windows under pad/vehicle turnaround capacity.
- Freeman, Moore, Schilling (1970) and Chamberlain & Kingsland (1970) are campaign-level parametric logistics /
  policy-comparison models; they are the ancestors of the campaign-design (multi-mission) network models.
- Gavish & Kalvenes (1997, MS) is the constellation-replenishment launch-policy DP: when to launch replacement
  satellites for a LEO constellation given finite lifetimes — a periodic-replenishment analogue for hub (constellation)
  capacity maintenance; outside the four venues.
- The modern methodology lineage (time-expanded generalized multicommodity network flow, event-driven networks,
  MILP campaign design, OOS logistics under demand uncertainty) is in Acta Astronautica / JSR / arXiv, not in OR, TS,
  TR-B or TR-C — the review should say so explicitly and position the spaceport feeder network design as importing
  OR/TS/TR-B hub-and-spoke, service-network-design and liner-feeder methodology into a problem that those venues have
  not yet treated.
